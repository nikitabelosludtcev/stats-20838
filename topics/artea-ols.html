Week 4 · reading OLS

The coupon coefficient in OLS

OLS, ordinary least squares, is a straight line here: by how many units Y rises when the coupon equals 1 rather than 0.

Solution slides. Artea solution. The slide rounds the coupon effect to +0.03 transactions (p = 0.03) and −$0.24 revenue (p = 0.72). The notebook is Lab_1_ArteaCase.

The model for the number of transactions:

trans_after = b₀ + b₁ × test_coupon + error

b₀ is the mean of Y when there is no coupon. b₁ is how much the mean of Y changes if the coupon was given. That is the treatment effect, in units of Y.

YIntercepttest_coupontp-value
Number of transactions, simple model0.13+0.032.210.03
Revenue, simple model7.78−0.24−0.360.72

The notebook is a little more precise than the slide, and that is normal. It is rounding.

  • Transactions: intercept 0.1257, coupon +0.0262, p = 0.027. R² = 0.001. The coupon explains a tiny share of the spread, but the mean still shifts significantly.
  • Revenue: intercept 7.7802, coupon −0.2415, p = 0.718. R² is about zero.

Read it out loud

  1. Without the coupon, a person makes about 0.13 transactions after the test window, on average. Most people buy zero times, so the mean is less than one.
  2. The coupon adds about 0.03 transactions per person. The slide writes “0.03 pp” for this shift. Because transactions are usually 0 or 1, the shift is close to +3 percentage points of the probability of a purchase. The safer sentence is: “plus 0.03 transactions, p = 0.03”.
  3. On revenue the effect is −0.24 dollars, p = 0.72. You cannot reject “there is no effect on revenue”. The slide says it that way: no significant effect on revenues.

A small R² does not cancel a significant shift in the mean. R² says what share of the differences between people is tied to the coupon. People differ a lot for other reasons. The average effect can still be real.

What happens if you add controls

In the notebook and on the slide the coupon stays in one model together with past purchases, minutes, the cart, and the channels. For transactions the coupon coefficient becomes 0.03, t = 2.38, p = 0.02. For revenue it is −0.28, t = −0.46, p = 0.64. The slide explains why the coupon barely moved: the assignment was random, so the other X’s should not change its coefficient much. They are there to describe, more precisely, who would have bought more anyway.

Those other coefficients are read this way because Y is a number, not an odds:

  • Each past purchase: about +0.06 transactions and +$3.17 of revenue.
  • An abandoned cart: about +0.17 transactions and +$9.31. These people are closer to a purchase even without the coupon.
  • Each extra week since the visit: about −0.02 transactions and −$1.11.
  • The channels Facebook, Instagram, Referral, and Other are positive relative to the Google baseline.
The conclusion they wantThe coupon causally raises the number of transactions by about 0.03. It did not raise average revenue per person in this test. “We can do better” is not the question “send it to everyone”. It is the question “send it to someone”.
A common missSeeing positive channel coefficients and saying “Instagram caused the purchases”. The channel was not assigned at random in this test. Only the coupon was random. The channel is a link. The coupon is closer to a cause.
