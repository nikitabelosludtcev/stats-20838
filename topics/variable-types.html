Week 1 · types of variables

Categories and numbers: why the type of a variable matters

The type decides which average is honest, which model you may use later, and which chart does not lie.

Lecture. Chiara Chiavenna, Data Analysis, part 1. Nominal, ordinal, discrete, and continuous, with the bank columns as the examples.

The slide splits variables into two families. Categorical stores groups. Numerical stores amounts you can add and divide.

TypeMeaningBank exampleWhat you must not do
NominalNames of groups, with no ordermarital: married, single, divorced. job: admin., blue-collar, student…Compute an “average marital status”
OrdinalGroups you can rankeducation: primary < secondary < tertiarySay tertiary is “so many euros more” than primary. The step size is unknown
DiscreteA count that jumps in whole numberscampaign: how many contacts in this campaign. previous: how many there were beforeTreat it as a yes/no scale
ContinuousA number that could, in principle, have a fractionbalance in euros, duration in seconds, age in yearsCall it a category only because the file happens to store whole numbers
The fork the rest of the course usesLater you choose the model from Y, not from fashion. Continuous spending → OLS. A purchase that is 0 or 1 → logit. That is this page, continued. Week 5 has the full “which Y, which model” table.

Why the type changes the calculation

Married appears 2,797 times out of 4,521. That is 61.9%. That is the mode, the most common category. The average “(married + single) / 2” does not exist. You cannot add words.

Age does add. The mean age is 41.2 years. Age is numerical.

Education is the trap. There is an order, and there is no distance. “Tertiary minus primary = 2” says nothing in euros. If the exam codes education as several 0/1 columns (primary yes/no, secondary yes/no), one level is left out as the baseline. The Red Bull mock does the same thing with age groups.

Worked labelling

  1. marital is nominal. Three names, and none is “more” than another.
  2. job is nominal. Twelve categories, including unknown.
  3. campaign is discrete. You cannot have 1.4 contacts.
  4. balance is continuous. It can be 1787, −88, or 0.
  5. duration is continuous. The file stores whole seconds, but it is a measurement of time.
  6. education is ordinal. The slide writes primary < secondary < tertiary. unknown is not a fourth step above tertiary. It means the information is missing.
A common missy (yes/no) looks like text, but a model codes it as 1 and 0. After that coding it is still a category with two levels, not a continuous “degree of agreement”. If the question is “the probability of yes”, you use logit, not OLS. In Artea the number of transactions stays a number, and they use OLS. The difference is what Y is.
