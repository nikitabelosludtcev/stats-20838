The slide’s three questions, already answered

  1. Predict restaurant stars on Google Maps. The slide’s answer: OLS, with the caveat “there are limitations”. Stars look like a number, but the scale is short and ordered. If the exam gives 1–5 and asks you to choose from a list, the course’s guide is OLS, and you should add the slide’s caveat rather than argue with it.
  2. Predict the number of likes. The answer: Poisson or negative binomial. Likes are a count, not yes/no and not euros.
  3. Predict the probability of a click on an ad. The answer: logit or probit. The outcome is 0/1.

In Red Bull the outcome “bought Red Bull, not another energy drink” is also 0/1, and the solution writes logistic regression outright. The column Exp(Coef.) next to it is another identifying mark of an odds ratio.

A common missSeeing transactions of 0, 1, 2 in Artea and demanding a logit. The lab honestly uses OLS, because Y is a count and revenue, not a probability. Logit would fit if Y were “bought / did not buy”. Both choices can be defended if you name what Y is.
