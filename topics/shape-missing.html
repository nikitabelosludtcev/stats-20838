Week 1 · shape and data quality

Skew, the box, and dirty data

Before you trust a coefficient, look at whether the column is broken. The course practises this on age and on balance.

If the mean sits clearly above the median, the right tail is long: a few large values. For age the shift is gentle, 41 against 39. For balance it is huge, 1,423 against 444. The slide says it plainly: under strong skew the mean can be a bad “typical” number.

What a boxplot is showing

The notebook draws a box for age and a box for balance. The box is built from the quartiles that describe() already printed.

Piece of the boxAge in the bankHow to read it
Lower whisker and minimum19 yearsThe youngest customer in the file
Bottom of the box, 25%33A quarter of customers are younger
Line inside, the median39The middle
Top of the box, 75%49Three quarters are younger
Top and the pointsmaximum 87A few older ages. On the balance chart those points run very far, up to 71,188

The slide does not write the formula for the whiskers. For the exam, name the median and the quartiles. Do not panic at a lone point until you have checked whether it is an error or a rare but real customer.

Worked example: €105,000

The slide’s “your turn” shows a suspicious amount of €105,000. Before you delete the row, the course asks four questions.

  1. A typing error? An extra zero, and it was €10,500 or €1,050.
  2. The wrong unit? Cents stored as euros, or the other way around.
  3. A duplicate? The same customer entered twice, so the amount is inflated.
  4. Is €105,000 actually on the original card? Then it is not rubbish. It is a rare customer. Deleting it pretends that such accounts do not exist.

In the bank file the maximum balance is €71,188 against a median of €444. That is a reason to open the row, not a reason to erase the tail automatically. If you delete every large account, the mean falls toward the median, and you have described a different, invented bank.

Missing values

Blank is not zero. The slide’s three questions:

  • the field was never collected;
  • the customer refused to answer;
  • the value was lost when the file was exported.

In later cases a missing value is sometimes stored as zero plus a separate flag. Pilgrim has age and agemiss, inc and incmiss. If age is unknown, the age column holds 0, and agemiss = 1 says “this is not a baby, this is a missing value”. A zero without the flag would be a lie. You will need that trick in week 7. Remember the device now.

On the examIf a table has a variable whose name ends in “miss”, do not read a zero in the main column as a real zero until you have seen the flag.
