Week 1 · hypothesis testing

Does the manager’s claim fit the data?

A hypothesis is a precise claim about the population. The data either live with it or they do not. They do not “prove the manager”.

The manager says: “our customers are 41 years old on average”. The file says 41.17. There is a gap. The slide’s question is whether that gap is bigger than ordinary noise.

The three steps on the slide 1. Write H₀, a precise claim about a parameter. Here H₀: μ = 41.
2. Measure how far x̄ sits from that claim, in units of noise.
3. Decide. Compatible → do not reject H₀. Too strange → reject H₀.

The rival, for a two-sided test, is H₁: μ ≠ μ₀. You care about a difference in either direction.

The claim “41 years”, with the arithmetic

  1. Observed gap: 41.17 − 41 = 0.17 years.
  2. Ordinary noise of the mean: SE = 0.157.
  3. Standardised gap: t = 0.17 / 0.157 = 1.08.
  4. For a two-sided test at α = 0.05, with a large sample, the guide is about ±1.96 SE. 1.08 sits inside that corridor.

The notebook and the slide both say: do not reject H₀. Age 41 is compatible with the data. The same conclusion through the interval: 41 lies between 40.86 and 41.48.

The sentence they take marks for“Do not reject H₀ does not mean we proved the claim true.” Not rejecting “41” does not prove the mean is exactly 41. It only shows that 41 does not look wild next to this sample.

The same data, a different claim

“Our customers are 44 on average.” H₀: μ = 44.

The interval is unchanged: 40.86 … 41.48. The number 44 is outside it.

t = (41.17 − 44) / 0.157 = −2.83 / 0.157 ≈ −18. That is far beyond −1.96.

The slide’s decision: reject H₀. The claim is not compatible with the data. The strength of the evidence changed because the claim changed, not because the file changed. That is the slide titled “same data, different claim”.

A second pair: a balance of €1,000

The slide asks, without finishing the sum, whether a mean balance of €1,000 is plausible when the sample mean is €1,423. The recipe is the same. The notebook has the SD of balance: 3,009.64, and n = 4,521.

  1. SE = 3009.64 / √4521 ≈ 3009.64 / 67.24 ≈ 44.76 euros.
  2. Gap: 1422.66 − 1000 = 422.66.
  3. t ≈ 422.66 / 44.76 ≈ 9.4. That is much further than 1.96.

Reject H₀: μ = 1000. A thousand euros cannot be the mean of all customers, given these data. The typical customer is still closer to the median of €444. Mean and median answer different questions.

Later, a p-value in OLS or logit is a cousin of this t. A small p-value means: a gap this large, or larger, fits badly with “there is no effect” plus random noise. In the course solutions the usual threshold is 0.05. The mock also comments on 0.10.
