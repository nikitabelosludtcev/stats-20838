Week 8 · the mock’s question on Lemon & Verhoef

Four kinds of touchpoint

On the slides the customer path is cut into “before the purchase, the purchase, after”. The four owners of a touchpoint live in detail in a paper. The folder has only the exam question and a pointer to that paper.

The closing class puts mapping the customer journey first among the course’s outcomes: behaviour and touchpoints, the places where a person meets the brand. The cases lay actions out in time: acquisition before the purchase, development and retention after. That is enough not to confuse Artea’s coupon with Pilgrim’s retention letter.

The mock then asks for a different cut, not by time but by who owns the point. The question, word for word: which touchpoints are interactions that are designed and managed by the firm and are under its control? The options:

  • A. Partner-owned
  • B. Customer-owned
  • C. Brand-owned
  • D. Social / external / independent

The solution file does not circle a letter. It writes: the answer rests on Lemon and Verhoef, “Understanding Customer Experience Throughout the Customer Journey”. By the definition already sitting inside the question, C fits, brand-owned: the point belongs to the brand and the brand manages it. A partner point is managed by the partner, a customer point by the person, and a social point by the outside world, not by the firm.

A boundaryThe full map of the paper is not in the uploaded PDFs. Do not learn, from this site, stages of experience, formulas from the paper, or figures you have not seen. If the paper is required on your syllabus, open it separately. Here, keep only the fork the mock has already shown, and the time axis from the slides.

How not to confuse this with Artea’s channel

channel_Instagram in Artea is where the person first arrived. That is an acquisition channel, a measurable column. A touchpoint in the question is a wider word: any contact, including one the firm does not own. On the exam, if they ask for the type of point, answer about the owner. If they ask for a case variable, answer with the column’s name.
