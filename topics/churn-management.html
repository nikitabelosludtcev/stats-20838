Week 5 · churn management

Targeted and untargeted churn

Churn is the share who left during a period, or the probability that one person leaves. The action “keep them” can be aimed or general, ahead of time or after the fact.

Two units of analysis sit on one slide. For person i: cᵢ is the probability they leave in this period. For the firm: c is the share of the base that left, and c = 1 − r, where r is the retention rate.

Why people leave, the slide’s list, which you should not extend on your own: value to the customer (quality, fit with the need, satisfaction, price), inertia and switching costs, and psychological and physical reasons.

Targeted, aimed at a personUntargeted, on everyone or on the setting
Proactive, ahead of timeFind the risky ones and get an offer to them in time. Pilgrim tests this cellRaise satisfaction, or the cost of leaving, with an action that does not aim at a list of “about to leave”. Books R Us tests this cell: teach new customers to buy in several channels
Reactive, after the factWait for the signal “not renewing”, then make an offer to stayThe cell is named on the slide. There are no separate numbers under it

The aimed path is forecast, then action. The slide draws the modelling process. First the manager’s question. Then which data are needed. Then the behaviour you predict, and the table. Then the choice of X, the choice of technique for the type of Y, estimation, a check on new people, and targeting by individual scores and by lift.

The sentence that separates a high markWho is inclined to leave, and whom a particular offer will keep, are not one question. A churn model answers the first. A field test answers the second. The Cases deck says it outright: churn model ≠ field experiment. Ascarza’s 2018 paper is titled Retention futility: aiming at the riskiest can be useless. The paper itself is not in the folder. Pilgrim shows the same logic in numbers. Study Pilgrim in detail in week 7.

Profit of a targeted action, as on the slide

The slide’s letters, without extra algebra that is not on the slide:

  • N is the number of customers.
  • α is the probability that a customer is touched by the programme.
  • β is the probability that they would have been leaving anyway.
  • ψ is the probability that the incentive reaches someone who was not going to leave.
  • Δ is the gain in LTV among the non-leavers who received the incentive anyway.

The slide draws that profit is made of saved leavers and of a side effect on people who would have stayed anyway, and that the incentive is not free. There is no single ready line “profit = …” in the slide text. It is a diagram. On the exam it is safer to name the letters and state the idea: part of the budget misses the people who are leaving, and an offer can even hurt if it disturbs a habit. Take the numerical verdict from Pilgrim. Do not invent a formula on top of the diagram.
