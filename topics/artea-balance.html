Week 4 · step 1 and step 2

The groups look alike. The raw means are not yet the answer

Check the random assignment before you celebrate a gap in sales. Otherwise you will praise the coupon for something else: more active people landed in one group.

Solution slides. Artea solution, steps 1 and 2, checked against the notebook Lab_1_ArteaCase. Where the notebook prints more decimals, the page says so.

Step 1. Were the groups alike before the coupon?

Compare means of features the coupon could not yet have changed: past purchases, the last receipt, how long since a visit, minutes, the cart, and the channels.

By eye the groups are close. The notebook does not fully trust the eye, and it runs a test. For the number of past purchases, t = −0.99 and the p-value = 0.32. For the Facebook share, a z-test, p-value = 0.33. Both p-values are large: a gap like this is easy to get by chance. These two checks are not a reason to shout “randomization broke”.

How to say it on the exam“The pre-test means are close, and the formal comparisons do not show a significant difference. So a later gap in purchases can be cautiously attributed to the coupon, not to the treatment group being a different kind of person.” One column with p = 0.32 does not replace looking at the whole table. The solution shows the whole table for that reason.

Step 2. Model-free evidence

Now look at the outcomes, after the test.

ControlCouponGap on the slide
Mean number of transactions13% on the picture; in the regression the base is 0.1315%about +2 percentage points
Mean revenue per person$7.78$7.54−$0.24

The slide itself asks: are these gaps significant, and how large is the error of the estimate? The raw picture does not say. Plus two points can be noise. That is why step 3 is a regression or a t-test, where the gap has a p-value.

The notebook notes a useful small point: a t-test of the difference in means and a simple regression Y ~ test_coupon give the same conclusion. For transactions, t = 2.207 and p = 0.027. That is not new magic. It is the same arithmetic.

A common missSeeing $7.54 < $7.78 and writing “the coupon reduced revenue”. A gap of −0.24, next to the noise you will see on the next page, is tiny. A direction without a p-value is not yet a result.
