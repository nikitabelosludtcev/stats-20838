The slide’s text, compressed into working sentences.

  • Constant 6.173, p = 0.793: the baseline level of profit when the other X’s are zero. It does not differ significantly from zero. In practice “every X is zero” is often a meaningless point, so do not turn the intercept into advice for the director.
  • Marketing Spend +1.305, p < 0.001: holding the rest fixed, one extra unit of spend is associated with about +$1.3 of profit.
  • Age +0.199, p = 0.011: one extra year is associated with +0.199 of profit.
  • Income +0.096, p < 0.001: one extra dollar of income is associated with +0.096 of profit.
  • The product +0.001, p = 0.034: the link between advertising and profit is stronger for richer customers.

All of these are “holding the rest fixed”. This is not a field test. The slide does not call the spending randomly assigned. The word associated is on the slide on purpose. Do not write “advertising caused” if there was no randomization.

Pseudo R² = 0.056. The slide reads it this way.

  • Intercept −0.667, p = 0.358: the baseline log-odds when every X is zero. Not significant. It does not go into the advice.
  • Age 0.001, p = 0.120: no link is visible. An OR of about 1.00 agrees with a tiny coefficient.
  • Female: OR 1.46, p = 0.019. The odds of the outcome are about 46% higher than for the baseline (the slide names the baseline male), holding the rest fixed. (1.46 − 1) × 100 = 46.
  • Discount: OR 1.39, p = 0.026. The odds are about 39% higher.
  • Mobile: OR 0.68, p = 0.019. The odds are about 32% lower, because (1 − 0.68) × 100 = 32.
A common missPseudo R² = 0.056 does not cancel three significant coefficients. And it does not sound like “we explained 5.6% of customers’ lives”.

The order of a paragraph on the exam

  1. Name the model and Y. Why OLS, or why logit.
  2. Name the baseline of each dummy: who is hidden inside the intercept.
  3. For each variable they ask about: the sign, the p-value, and a translation into units of Y or into percent of the odds.
  4. The interaction separately, if there is one.
  5. One sentence on cause: was there an experiment? If not, “associated”, not “caused”.
