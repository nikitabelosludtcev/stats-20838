Week 7 · association versus experiment

One table, two different questions

Almost every case in the course puts a fat observed gap next to a thin effect of a random action. Mixing them up is the typical way to lose the case.

Across the cases. Not a separate lecture. The pairs come from Cases 3–4 (viral texts, Pilgrim, Books R Us), the Artea solution, and Matching 2026.

CaseThe observed linkThe random action
Viral textsWhich emotions appear in articles people shareTwo assigned levels of amusement, 2.92 against 3.97
ArteaPast purchases and the channel are linked with future revenueOnly the coupon. Its coefficient barely moves when controls are added
PilgrimThe model says who looks like a leaverThe offer inside deciles 1–3. On average, a minus for retention
Books R UsAn already multichannel customer: about −74% on the odds of churn, and +€33 of profitThe MNF letter: about €1, and a churn OR of 0.89
Sample BoxA raw spending gap of €199There was no assignment. After matching about €41 remains, and that is still an estimate under assumptions

A worked paragraph

Question. “Multichannel customers bring €33 more. Let us send everyone a letter about channels and budget +€33 per person.”

  1. €33 compares people who already behave differently. An active customer both chooses the channel and leaves the money. That is not the effect of the letter’s text.
  2. The letter in the test shifts the multichannel share by about one point and profit by about €1. A budget of €33 is too high by tens of times.
  3. Even €1 still has to be compared with the cost of the mailing. The slide says it outright: a candidate to validate and evaluate against costs.
  4. The right recommendation: scale MNF cautiously, not MF and not the value message with a cash incentive, and do not promise the historical channel gap.

The same template carries over to the mock about Early Access. People who signed up early spend €58 against €13. The gap of €45 is not the effect of the programme while signup is voluntary. That is on the mock page.
