Week 4 · HBR Artea

Artea: a coupon tested by an experiment

A clothing shop can see how people move around the site, and it is bad at turning a visit into a purchase. The test is a random 20% coupon.

Case slides. Artea (HBR, Ascarza and Israeli): the lab slides that set up the 20% coupon. The full Harvard case text is not in the folder.

The case is from Harvard Business Review, Eva Ascarza and Ayelet Israeli. The solution header says April 2021. The closing-class reading list says 25 September 2020. For the exam, the lab’s numbers matter, not the mismatch of dates on the cover. The industry is clothing and accessories. The company is already data driven: there is a data science team and a dashboard. CEO Alex Campbel’s problem: many people who were on the site never bought. Engagement looks fine. Conversion is weak.

The action: a 20% discount coupon to registered users. This is a field test, not a survey that asks “would you like a discount?”.

FactNumber
Website users in the test5,000
Received the coupon, treatment2,502
Did not receive it, control2,498
The manager’s questionsDid transactions rise? Did spending per person rise? Who should get the coupon next?

What sits in one row

ColumnMeaning
idA code for the person
trans_afterHow many transactions after the experiment. One of the Y’s
revenue_afterRevenue in dollars after the experiment. The second Y
test_coupon1 if the coupon was sent, 0 if not. This is the X that was assigned
num_past_purchHow many purchases there were before
spent_last_purchaseDollars spent last time
weeks_since_visitWeeks since the last visit
browsing_minutesMinutes on the site at the last visit
shopping_cart1 if, on the last visit, they put an item in the cart and did not buy
channel_Facebook, Instagram, Referral, Other1/0 for where the person first arrived. If all four are zero, the solution treats the channel as Google. Google is the baseline, and it has no column of its own

The channel is the first touch, when the person became a prospect, not necessarily a buyer yet. That is last week’s acquisition vocabulary.

The four steps the lab asks for

  1. Open Artea / DataArtea.
  2. Check the random assignment: the groups should look alike before the test.
  3. Model-free evidence: simply compare the means of trans_after and revenue_after. “Model-free” means “no regression yet”.
  4. Check the effect with a regression. Then heterogeneity: does the coupon help everyone equally?
Keep the case’s two questions apart“Did the coupon work on average?” and “who should receive it?” need different tables. The average effect can be small while a slice of the audience has a large one. That is the whole moral of the wrap-up: heterogeneity pays.
