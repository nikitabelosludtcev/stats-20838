Week 2 · set and dict

Intersection, keys, and zip

A set answers “who fits every condition”. A dictionary answers “what is written under this name”.

Presentation. Python Basics. Sets, dictionaries, and the intersection that leaves Diane.

Intersection keeps only what is shared

The slide’s puzzle: the killer VictorThePlum must have motive, opportunity, and means. In set language, the person must sit in all three sets.

staff = {"Diane", "Eleanor", "Kassandra", "Jacob"}
in_studio = {"Eleanor", "Jack", "Diane"}
smokers = {"Jacob", "Diane", "Kassandra"}
suspects = staff & in_studio & smokers

The sign & is the intersection. Diane is in every set. Eleanor is not among the smokers. Jacob is not in the studio. Diane remains. The Olympics food puzzle uses the same move on three sets of favourite food and, after sorted, prints ['pizza', 'tacos'].

SignNameA plain meaning
&IntersectionFriends who are free on Friday and on Saturday
|UnionFree on Friday or on Saturday, on at least one of the days
-DifferenceFriends minus the busy ones: neither = friends − (friday | saturday)
<=Subsetcriteria.issubset(option): every requirement is inside the flat you are considering
A common missThe union | does not mean “fits every criterion”. It gathers everyone who landed in at least one set. For “every condition is met” you need the intersection, or issubset.

A dictionary: the key leads to the value

best_grades = {"Kirs": 29, "Paula": 30}
print(best_grades["Kirs"])  # 29

A key is unique. In the Kitchen Crew exercise there are six employees and their roles, and two of the roles are Chef. The lines

kitchen = dict(zip(roles, employees))
print(len(employees), len(kitchen))  # 6 5
print(kitchen["Chef"])  # Joelle Bastianich

do this. zip glues two queues into pairs. dict keeps one Chef: the second write overwrites the first. So there are 6 people and 5 keys. Who remains Chef depends on who comes later in the list. The slide fixes the answer as Joelle Bastianich, so in their order she is the last person with that role.

zip stops at the shorter list

people = ["Babette", "Karen", "Jo", "Linda", "Janne"]
pets = ["dog", "cat", "dog", "dog", "ozelot", "anaconda"]
pet_lookup = dict(zip(people, pets))
print(len(pet_lookup))  # 5

There are six animals and five people. The extra anaconda is given to nobody. zip does not stretch the short list, and it does not raise an error. It simply stops.

Worked example: the flat

Criteria: two bedrooms, elevator, metro nearby, nice neighborhood. One option: 3 bedrooms, elevator, tram, nice area.

  1. Turn both descriptions into sets of requirement-words.
  2. Ask criteria <= option, that is “is every criterion inside the option?”
  3. Metro nearby is not in the option. There is a tram. Two bedrooms is not there either. There are 3 bedrooms. The test fails.
  4. The option the slide marks MEETS EVERY CRITERION passes issubset. A “nearly the same” neighbourhood, or a tram instead of the metro, does not.
