Week 7 · Case 3

Pilgrim Bank: a targeted offer made retention worse

The bank found the people who looked like leavers and made them a generous offer. On average, retention fell. This is the central surprise of the course.

31,634 customers. An internal model at the start gives each person a probability of churn and cuts the base into 10 deciles. Decile 1 is the highest predicted risk. Decile 10 is the lowest. Only deciles 1–3 go into the test, 9,490 people. A year later they look at who stayed, across the whole base, but the offer’s effect is computed only inside the test.

The most expensive mistake in the caseCompare the people who received the offer with everyone who did not, including the 22,144 outside the test. Outside the test the risk is lower by construction. They are not a random control. The slide writes: missing treatment is not control.

Checks first

Lift across deciles: observed churn is higher where the model promised high risk. The ranking works. A check of the balance of the assignment: p = 0.893, and no joint tilt in the covariates was found. Randomness inside deciles 1–3 looks decent.

The average effect

The share still there after a year: control 65.20%, offer 63.13%. The gap is −2.07 percentage points. Inside this risky group, the offer is on average associated with worse retention.

Logit, Y = retention, N = 9,490. Pseudo R² = 0.074. The coefficient on target_proactive = −0.1008, p = 0.0247.

OR = exp(−0.1008) = 0.904.

The odds of staying are lower by (1 − 0.904) × 100 ≈ 9.6%.

The slide reminds you separately: “about 10%” refers to the odds, and the raw gap in probabilities is −2.07 points. Both numbers are true, and they are about different things.

Beside them in the same model, as controls, not as an experiment: online banking before, +0.199 (p = 0.008), and each year of tenure, +0.059 (p < 0.001). Missing age and missing income have large negative coefficients. Those are the flags agemiss and incmiss again, not “a baby with age 0”.

For newer customers the offer is on the edge of a plus. For long tenure the minus is strong. The slide offers a possible explanation, “broken inertia”: an anxious call woke a person who was staying out of habit. That is a hypothesis. It must be tested. Do not present it as a found fact. Before you declare heterogeneity proved, the slide asks for an interaction of the offer and tenure, not only two separate little tables.

The decision for the manager

  • Do not scale the offer to the whole risky base. The average effect in the test is negative.
  • Do not confuse “high risk” with “a positive response to this package of gifts”.
  • It makes sense to narrow the audience, starting with shorter tenure, and to test again, rather than to trust one subgroup with p = 0.051.
  • Do not pronounce pseudo R² = 0.074 as “the model explained 7% of retention” in the sense of an OLS variance.

Practice

Who must not be treated as the control in Pilgrim?

The offer’s coefficient is −0.1008. Type the OR rounded to three decimals, and the percent reduction in the odds as a whole number, as on the slide.
