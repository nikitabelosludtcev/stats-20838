Week 7 · Case 4

Books R Us: the letter matters more than the multichannel fact in a regression

Historically, people who use several channels are more profitable. That does not yet mean a letter saying “use several channels” will create the same €33.

The bookseller sells in store, by mail, by phone, on the site, and in the app. Membership renews once a year, so churn here is observed: they did not renew. Historical cohorts 1 and 2 show a link. Cohort 3 is already a field test on new customers who live in a store’s area.

Why the new ones. Their channel habit is weaker, so a letter can still move behaviour. The slide’s four questions: can marketing create multichannel customers; will they be more profitable than they would have been without the letter; what happens to churn; which text works.

What was there before the experiment

In the history, being multichannel is associated with +€20.95 of profit a year, with controls. In a logit of churn the coefficient is −1.0227. That is an association in cohort 1, N about 35,391 on one of the slides. You cannot read it as an effect of the letter: the person chose the channel themselves.

A separate regression, already on the field file but still about the observed channel, not about the letter: multichannel in the churn logit has coefficient −1.3417, OR = exp(−1.3417) ≈ 0.26, so the odds of leaving are about 74% lower. In the profit regression the coefficient is +€32.58. N = 30,710. The slide stresses: channel use is observed, not randomly assigned.

Historical CLV uses CLV = m(1+d)/(1+d−r) − AC, with d = 0.12 and AC = €10. The bars: multichannel €97.19, one channel €30.85. You cannot plug in someone else’s m. The slide already gives the finished CLVs.

The five groups in the test

The letter goes out in period 5 of the acquisition year, and purchases are watched after that. The names: MNF and MF invite people to use several channels. VPNF and VPF tell the company’s value. F means a financial incentive. NF means without one.

Why €1 and €33 do not argue

€32.58 is the gap between people who became multichannel on their own and people who did not. Among the first group there are more people who were active anyway.

€1.04 is the gap between people who were randomly sent the MNF letter and the control. The letter moves the channel only a little, so the money is small too.

A random letter does not randomly assign the future channel. The channel after the letter is already a response, not the treatment.

The mechanism the slide finds plausible: MNF explains how to buy in different channels, and it does not add a cash incentive. A survey after the test, 2,068 answers, and a separate communication experiment support this qualitatively. The exact 1–5 scales on the figure did not come out in the text. The mark depends on the idea, not on the height of a bar.

Extra checks on the slide: the multichannel profit gap remains inside the same purchase frequency, and it is not explained only by a mix of cheap channels. That is an argument against “they simply visit more often”. For the exam it is enough to remember that the channel’s association is fat, the letter’s effect is thin, and the case measured both.

The sentence“The historical phase and the Cohort 3 experiment are kept apart.” If an answer calls €33 the effect of the mailing, the mark comes off.
