Week 3 · Customer Acquisition

Customer acquisition: the first touch and the first money

Acquisition is not “any advertising”. In this course it is the moment a person buys or subscribes for the first time.

Presentation. Customer Acquisition. The slide’s definition, citing Gupta and Zeithaml (2006) and Schweidel et al. (2008), plus the KPI names. The journey is named again in the closing class.

The definition on the slide, citing Gupta and Zeithaml (2006) and Schweidel et al. (2008): the first time a new customer buys from the firm or subscribes to a service. Next to it sits an earlier touch: the firm’s first contact with the person, for example a website registration or agreeing to a catalogue. Artea is exactly about people who are already registered and still have not bought.

The slide warns against the simple idea “scale whichever campaign brings the most sales”. Sales without the cost of acquiring the customer, and without the quality of that customer, easily go negative. Two abbreviations appear beside it.

  • CLV, customer lifetime value: how much margin the customer will bring over the whole time with the firm. The formula is week 5.
  • CAC, customer acquisition cost: what it cost to bring them in.

Data help when they find a combination of high CLV and low CAC. What you can measure even before the purchase: the fact of the first purchase itself, behaviour before it (search, minutes on the site), and a marketing touch at the person level.

The flow, and the KPIs

The slide draws a chain: notice an intention → contact → deliver → measure and improve. KPIs are laid out by goal. Do not mix them.

GoalExamples on the slideIn plain English
Prospectingnew customers, qualified leads, reach, CTR, CPCDid you reach people, and what did a click cost?
ConversionCPA/CPO, cost per visit, CVR, ROAS, revenue and margin of the first orderWhat did the action cost, and did it pay back?
Store impactshare of bookings, show-rate, extra visitsDid they actually reach the store?
Qualityreturns, cancellations, lead validityWas the “purchase” real?
ExperienceNPS, first-contact resolutionWhat was the first experience like?

CTR is the share of impressions that became a click. CPC is the price of a click. CVR is the share of clicks or visits that became a purchase. ROAS is revenue divided by advertising spend. CPA is the price of the target action. The course does not want an internet definition. It wants the habit of not praising a campaign for its CTR when the CPA is huge and the customers return the goods.

Performance marketing, on the slide, is measurable actions (a click, a purchase, a booking) and a constant retuning of the creative and the targeting. It sounds easy. The next slide puts it on the ground: the data are usually patchy, and you cannot hand-label “looks like a good customer” for thousands of people. That is why statistical models come next. One line of self-defence from the lecturer: do not hand your data over as carelessly as you would bargain for a carpet while wearing a Rolex. That is a picture, not a formula.

The customer journey

The closing class calls the first outcome of the course the ability to see the path and the touchpoints, the places where a person meets the brand. In the cases the path is cut into three pieces: before the purchase, the purchase, and after the purchase. Acquisition lives at the start. Development and retention live after. The four types of touchpoints from Lemon & Verhoef are not spelled out on the slides, but the mock asks for them by name. A short page in week 8 says honestly that the paper itself is not in the folder.

The link to ArteaThe case’s question is acquisition: a 20% coupon for people registered on a clothing site who often never buy. Before you read a coefficient, name Y (a purchase, or revenue after the test) and X (the coupon).
