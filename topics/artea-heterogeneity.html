Week 4 · heterogeneity

An interaction, and who to target

The average effect hides pieces that point in opposite directions. The coupon does not help “a user in general”. It clearly helps some channels, and the people who abandoned a cart.

An interaction

The regression adds the product “channel × coupon”. That product is 1 only for a person who both came from that channel and received the coupon. Then the bare coefficient on test_coupon is the coupon’s effect for the baseline channel, Google. The coefficient on Instagram & Coupon is how much the Instagram effect differs from the Google effect.

How to say it. For Google there is no significant plus. For Instagram the effect on transactions is higher by 0.07 (p < 0.01), and the effect on revenue is higher by about $2.96 (p = 0.04). Facebook is on the edge of 10% for transactions (p = 0.07) and is not significant for revenue. Referral and Other do not differ from the baseline significantly.

The abandoned cart

A separate model puts in shopping_cart × coupon.

  • On transactions: the coefficient is 0.0846, p = 0.000.
  • On revenue: +2.997 dollars, p = 0.025.

The bare test_coupon is then almost zero: 0.0009, p = 0.945, for transactions. The average plus we saw earlier sits mostly among the people who had already put an item in the cart.

The slide’s bars show the same thing. With an abandoned cart, the gap “coupon versus no coupon” is visible. Without a cart, the bars are close.

A decision you can defend

  1. Do not mail the coupon to everyone. The average effect on revenue is not established, and on Google the raw means are even lower.
  2. It makes sense to aim at Instagram, and at people who abandoned a cart. There you have both a model-free gap and a significant interaction.
  3. The new-campaign file may contain extra fields. The slide asks you to choose a policy explicitly: nobody, everyone, only Facebook, only Instagram with no purchase history, and to think about whether demographics would help. Extra fields can be used for a portrait only if they were known before the coupon decision. Otherwise you are peeking at the outcome, not targeting.

A caveat: an interaction shows where the effect differs. It does not prove a permanent law that “Instagram is always better”. This is this test, this 20% discount, this site.

The sentence“Managing heterogeneity pays.” That is the last line of the Artea solution. On the mock, the same logic is asked as “who should receive the discount”.
