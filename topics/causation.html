Week 3 · causation

When a gap between two groups is not yet a cause

Without an experiment, the course lets you speak only of an association, a link. A cause needs three conditions and an honest control.

Presentation. Customer Acquisition. Three conditions for a cause, the field-test definition, and the packaging shares 0.60 and 0.40. The Berger and Milkman illustration (2.92 against 3.97) is one slide in Cases 3–4.

The slide offers two everyday claims. “Paracetamol reduces a headache.” “A red cloth angers a bull.” It asks which one is causal, and why. The answer does not rest on intuition. It rests on three requirements.

Three conditions for saying X → Y 1. Temporal precedence: the change in X happens before the change in Y.
2. Covariance: there is an observed link between X and Y.
3. No plausible other explanation.

In an experiment the same three points look like this:

  • X happens before Y, and X is usually changed on purpose. That is manipulation.
  • The link is estimated by collecting data.
  • The other factors are held under control. Usually that is randomization, assignment by chance.

The slide’s definition of a field test, in its own words: a procedure in which one or more treatments are changed, an observed outcome is then collected (choice, spending, purchase frequency), and whatever could distort the result is controlled, for example people’s characteristics. The last line of the slide: without experimentation there is association, but not causation.

The packaging example

Two colours of pack, and the choice of brand. On the slide the shares are: the blue pack is chosen in 0.60 of cases, the green pack in 0.40.

  • X, the independent variable: the colour. The researcher changes it.
  • O, the dependent variable: the choice. You judge whether the experiment worked by looking at O. The slide uses the letter O, not Y, but the role is the same as Y in a regression.
  • The treatment effect: 0.60 − 0.40 = 0.20, that is 20 percentage points. The slide writes “20%” to mean this difference of shares.

The difference becomes an effect of colour only if the groups did not already differ in something else. Otherwise it is selection bias.

A common miss20 percentage points and “the probability rose by 20%” are different sentences. Here 0.40 became 0.60, and the gap is 0.20. The relative rise would be 0.20 / 0.40 = 50%. The slide means the difference 0.60 − 0.40.

Where the course is almost an experiment, and where it is not

The Cases 3–4 deck briefly shows Berger and Milkman on viral New York Times articles. First, emotions in the text are linked with shares: that is observation, an association. Then two versions of a Jimmy Dean advertising story, with different levels of amusement, 2.92 against 3.97, and a p-value of 0.00: here the emotion is assigned. The paper itself is not in the file. This is a one-slide illustration. The point of the illustration: a forecast and an experiment answer different questions. “Which emotions appear in viral texts” is not “if we add humour, there will be more shares”.

The same fork later becomes the heart of Pilgrim. A churn model says who looks like someone about to leave. A field test says whether the offer will keep them.
