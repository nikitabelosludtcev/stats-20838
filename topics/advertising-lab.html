Week 6 · the logistic exercise

Advertising: from the share of HighSales to two strategies

200 observations, three advertising channels, and a binary outcome. The lab is a ready template for how to write an interpretation.

The file is Advertising_logistic.csv. The original continuous sales were removed, and HighSales = 1 was left if sales were above 15, otherwise 0. The columns are TV, radio, newspaper, and HighSales. The business question: which channels are linked with a high probability of high sales.

This is a link, not a field test. Nobody assigned advertising spend at random in this file. The managerial paragraph writes it that way: associated with, not causes.

Step 1. Look

advertising["HighSales"].value_counts()
advertising["HighSales"].mean()
advertising.plot(kind="scatter", x="TV", y="HighSales")

There are 125 zeros and 75 ones. The share is 75/200 = 0.375, that is 37.5%. The scatter of TV against 0/1: the ones sit more often on the right, where TV is large, but the clouds overlap. The link is positive and noisy. For a binary Y a scatter is honest, because you can see the overlap. A pretty straight line here would be a deception.

Each row is read with the other channels held fixed. Plus one unit of TV: the odds are 7.7% higher. Plus one unit of radio: 47.7% higher. Newspaper: the OR is about 1, the interval includes 1, p = 0.399, and no separate link is visible.

TV had an OR of 1.021 in the simple model, and here it is 1.077. The meaning of the coefficient changed: you are now comparing observations with the same radio and the same newspaper. This is not “TV became more important because we wanted it to”. It is a different question, with controls.

Step 6. Goodness of fit

Pseudo R² of the multiple model is about 0.825. The LLR p-value is 4.723×10⁻⁴⁷, so the three channels together beat a model with only an intercept. And an immediate ban from the solution: you must not say that pseudo R² means “the model explained 82.5% of the variance of HighSales”.

The notebook warns: possibly complete quasi-separation, about 39% of observations are predicted almost perfectly. That is where the huge intercept of −23 comes from, and why the two strategies have probabilities near 0 and near 1. On the exam, if you see that warning, say: the coefficients can still be interpreted, but the extreme probabilities are fragile. The lab solution still writes the ORs and the two probabilities. It does not throw the model away.

Steps 7–8. Two strategies, and a paragraph for the manager

The gap is about 0.999, that is 99.9 percentage points. Strategy B almost certainly lands in HighSales under this model. A almost certainly does not. The solution stresses: a gulf between probabilities is not the same thing as “the difference in odds ratios”. An OR describes a multiplier for one unit of a channel. A probability describes a particular bundle of spending.

A paragraph close to the solution’s sample. Higher spending on TV and on radio is associated with higher odds of HighSales, even if the other channels are held constant. For newspaper no separate link is visible: the OR interval contains 1. The two example strategies give completely different probabilities. These are associations, not a proven effect from a random experiment. Uncertainty is named through the p-values and the intervals.

What to have ready37.5%. Simple TV OR 1.021, and 1.233 for +10 units. P(TV=100) = 13.3%. Multiple model: TV 1.077, radio 1.477, newspaper 1.016 with p = 0.399. Pseudo R² 0.825 is not a share of variance.
