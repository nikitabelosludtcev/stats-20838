Week 1 · population, sample, standard error

From the sample to an unknown mean

4,521 customers are not yet “every customer of the bank”. The gap between those two phrases is inferential statistics.

The slide puts two questions side by side.

  • Descriptive statistics. What does the thing we already see look like?
  • Inferential statistics. What can the sample say about a wider population?
NameIn the age example
Population. A parameter, usually unknown. The population mean is written μEvery customer the manager wants a conclusion about
Sample. A statistic you can compute. The sample mean is written x̄The 4,521 people in the file, x̄ = 41.17

The same move for balance: x̄ = €1,423, and the true μ is unknown. The sample helps you learn μ, but with uncertainty attached.

Why the mean jumps

If you had drawn other customers from the same bank, the mean would have been different. The slide shows three possible mean ages: 40.78, 41.17, and 41.29. They are close, and they are not equal. That is sampling variability, the luck of the draw.

Two different “errors”
SD = 10.57 years. How much customers differ from each other.
SE(x̄) = SD / √n = 10.57 / √4521 = 0.157 years. How much the mean itself usually jumps from sample to sample.

The square root of 4,521 is about 67.2. Dividing by a large n means: with a large sample the mean sits still, even if the people are very different. The notebook checks this live. A mean of 50 random people jumps around. A mean of 4,000 barely moves.

The 95% interval, with the arithmetic written out

For a large sample the slide writes:

95% CI ≈ x̄ ± 1.96 × SE

  1. 1.96 × 0.157 = 0.308, which rounds to 0.31.
  2. Lower end: 41.17 − 0.31 = 40.86.
  3. Upper end: 41.17 + 0.31 = 41.48.

The notebook prints the more precise ends: 40.862 … 41.478. The slide rounds to 40.86 and 41.48.

The interval is the set of values of μ that look plausible with these data. The number 41 falls inside. The number 44 does not.

A common missOnce the interval has been computed, do not say “there is a 95% probability the true age is in here” as if the true age were still rolling a die. In this course you use the interval practically: a claim inside the interval is not rejected, and a claim outside it is rejected. And “not rejected” does not mean “we proved the manager right”.
