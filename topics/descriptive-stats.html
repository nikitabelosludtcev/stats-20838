Week 1 · descriptive statistics

How to describe a column in one number

Descriptive statistics answer only “what does our sample look like?”. They do not yet prove anything about every customer of the bank.

Lecture. Chiara Chiavenna, Data Analysis, part 1. Mean, median, mode, and the standard deviation. The same numbers are recomputed in the bank_basics notebook.

The slide gives four measures. Everyday speech calls all of them “the average”. They are not the same question.

MeasureWhat it measuresWhich variablesA course example
ModeWhat appears most oftenNominal, ordinal, discretemarried, 61.9%. Or, on the slide’s picture, “Instagram is the most common platform”
MedianThe middle, once the values are lined up. Half are below, half are aboveOrdinal and numericalOn the toy slide, median spend is €50. In the bank, median age is 39. Median balance is €444
MeanThe sum divided by the number of observations. The ordinary averageNumericalMean age 41.17. Mean balance €1,423
Standard deviation, SDHow far the values sit from their own meanNumericalSD of age is 10.57 years. On the slide’s example the SD of balance is about €3,045: two shops can have the same average sales and a very different day-to-day spread
Say the formula in words
mean = sum of the values / n
A large SD means the customers differ a lot from each other. The SD does not say how precise your estimate of the mean is. Precision is the standard error, and that is the next topic.

Age, number by number

The notebook prints:

  • n = 4,521
  • mean = 41.17
  • median = 39
  • SD = 10.58
  • minimum 19, maximum 87
  • quartiles: 25% of people are younger than 33, and 75% are younger than 49

The mean sits a little above the median. The tail of older ages pulls the mean up. The slide says: mean and median together hint at the shape. If the skew is strong, the mean is a bad picture of the “typical” customer.

Worked example: balance

balance: mean €1,422.66, median €444, SD €3,009.64, minimum −3,313, maximum 71,188.

  1. Half of the customers have no more than €444 in the account. That is the median, the person in the middle of the list.
  2. The mean is almost three times larger because a few very large accounts pull the sum up. The single maximum of 71,188 already moves it.
  3. An SD of about €3,000, next to a median of €444, means a huge spread. It does not mean “the typical customer, plus or minus three thousand”.
  4. If a manager asks for the “ordinary balance” in these data, the honest answer is the median. The mean answers a different question: how many euros per customer if you add every account together.
A common missPeople treat the SD as “the error of the study”. The SD describes how customers differ. Later, SE = SD / √n describes how much the mean would jump if you drew another sample of the same size.
