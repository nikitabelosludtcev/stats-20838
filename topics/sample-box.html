Week 7 · Protein & Fitness

Sample Box: €199 raw, €41 after the pairs, and a pilot in the red

A box of products looked like a gold mine while recipients were compared with everyone else. Similar people spent almost as much.

Presentation. Matching 2026, the Protein & Fitness Sample Box. Raw gap about €199, ATT €40.97. The interval €34.63–€47.31 is the notebook’s; the slide flags it as a paired t-test.

January 2022, the online shop Protein & Fitness. Loyal customers were sent a box matched to their sport and to past touches. This is a pilot, not a random test: 5,000 customers, 1,800 received the box (36%), and 3,200 did not (64%). The money window is spending in 2022. Everything used to explain the selection comes from 2021.

A portrait of all 5,000: age 44 (SD 15.1), women 45.3%, purchases in 2021 on average 16.6, spending €393, an app for 69.6%. Countries: Italy 40.2%, Spain 30.5%, UK 29.2%. Sport: gym 40%, running 18.8%, yoga 15.6%, swimming 11%, cycling 9.8%, other 4.8%.

Why the raw €199 lies

Mean spending in 2022: €295.04 without the box, €493.98 with the box. The gap is +€198.94. Before the programme the recipients were already different.

They were the kind of people who spend more anyway. Part of the €199 is them, not the box.

The pairs

A logit on the 2021 characteristics gives a score. The mean score is 0.287 for those without the box and 0.490 for those with it. There is overlap, but the recipients are shifted to the right. The important predictors of selection on the slide: purchases, spending, past samples, guides, challenges, the app.

Then 1,800 pairs: each recipient was given a control. There are only 985 unique controls. Some are used more than once. That is matching with replacement. After the pairing, the standardized differences on age, purchases, spending, samples, subscription, and the app are below 0.10. Balance on these X’s became decent.

The outcome comparison: €493.98 against €453.01 for the matched control. ATT = +€40.97. The notebook’s interval is €34.63–€47.31, p < 0.001. The slide’s asterisk warns: this is a paired t-test. Controls that were reused need a careful estimate of uncertainty. The interval is a guide, not the last statistical word.

Most of the raw gap, about €158 of the €199, was the composition of the group. The remaining €41 is still significantly above zero. On this estimate the box raised recipients’ spending, only much more modestly than it looked.

The economics of the pilot

The box costs €18, including the goods, the packing, and delivery. Gross margin on the extra sales is 40%.

  1. Extra revenue: 40.97 × 1,800 ≈ €73,747.
  2. Gross profit: 73,747 × 0.40 ≈ €29,499.
  3. Cost: 18 × 1,800 = €32,400.
  4. Result: 29,499 − 32,400 = −€2,901, about −€1.61 per recipient.
  5. Break-even: 18 / 0.40 = €45 of extra spending. An ATT of €41 does not clear that bar.

Spending rose, and the pilot is loss-making at the average estimate. The spread across pairs is huge, from large minuses to large pluses. The average of €41 is not the effect for every person.

Above €45 you see people without the app, people with high past spending, Spain, Italy, running, and cycling. The slide says: these are candidates for a new test, not a finished policy. Separate averages do not prove the effect of their intersection, for example “a Spanish runner with no app”. The tests on the slide: the app, value, country, and sport have p < 0.001. Age does not.
