Week 6 · interactions

When the effect is different for different people

An interaction is not “one more ordinary plus”. It says that the slope of one X depends on another X.

You have already seen three of them. In Artea the coupon did almost nothing for baseline Google, and it clearly moved Instagram and the abandoned cart. In the closing-class OLS, advertising is more strongly linked with profit among the rich. In the Red Bull mock, the promo is linked with brand choice differently for the young and for men. The rule is one rule.

Effect of X in group D = coefficient of X + coefficient of (X × D), when D = 1
When D = 0, only the coefficient of X remains

So the bare coefficient of X, once an interaction is in the model, is not the effect “on average across the hospital”. It is the effect in the baseline group, where every D equals zero.

The arithmetic, on Artea’s numbers

For transactions, in the model with channels: test_coupon = −0.01 (p = 0.51), and Instagram × coupon = +0.07 (p < 0.01).

  1. A person from Google, every channel dummy = 0. The coupon’s effect is about −0.01. p = 0.51, so it does not differ from zero.
  2. A person from Instagram. The coupon’s effect is about −0.01 + 0.07 = +0.06 transactions. The extra 0.07 is significant.

You cannot say “the coupon is insignificant” by looking only at the bare test_coupon. It is insignificant for the baseline. The manager’s question “whom to send it to” lives in the interaction row.

How not to mangle a percent

In OLS an interaction of 0.001 is already in units of profit, per unit of advertising and per unit of income. A small number can matter if income is measured in dollars rather than in thousands: for every $1,000 of income the slope of advertising rises by 1. Look at the units. The slide itself does not translate 0.001 into “a very weak effect”. It speaks only of the sign and the significance.

In a logit the interaction also lives in log-odds. You compute a percent from exp of the sum, if you need the group’s overall multiplier, or from exp of the interaction itself, if you need “how much the group’s multiplier differs from the baseline”. The Red Bull solution takes the second, shorter path: a significant positive interaction means the link between the promo and the odds of choosing Red Bull is stronger in that group than in the baseline. They do not multiply every OR into one probability.

A common missReading an interaction as the main effect. The row Promo × Male does not say “men choose Red Bull more often”. That is what the row Male says. The product row says that the coupon is linked with choice differently for men than for the baseline.
