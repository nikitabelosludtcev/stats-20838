Course 20838 · a.y. 2026–2027

Eight weeks until the marketing analytics exam

Course 20838 teaches you to make a marketing decision from a table, not from a feeling. These pages walk through the uploaded lectures, labs, and practice exam in the same order the slides use. Every term is unpacked in plain English. The numbers are the course numbers. The English names stay exactly as they appear on the slides: odds ratio, p-value, lift chart, field test, propensity score.

75 minutes

The written exam. For an attending student it is 50% of the grade, and the project is the other 50%. The mock has a short case with open questions and multiple choice. One point for a correct MCQ, zero for a wrong one.

How a high mark is earned On a case the marker checks four things. Which model is this, and why? Which coefficient is significant? What does it mean in odds or in euros? Can you call it a cause? The sentence “the coefficient is positive, so raise the price” is exactly the sentence that loses marks.

How a week is built

A workable load if you have not studied statistics before and you want a high mark: about 9–11 hours a week.

Mon, Wed, Fri
70–90 minutes. One topic, one numerical example, then the practice until the check turns green.
Tue, Thu
50–70 minutes. A second topic, or say yesterday’s formulas out loud with the notes closed.
Sat
2.5–3 hours. Put the week back together on one case (Artea, Advertising, Pilgrim, or Sample Box) and say the conclusion as if to a manager.
Sun
Rest, or 20 minutes on the formula sheet. Do not open a new topic on Sunday.

Day by day

Each word is defined before it is used. The mock exam sits at the end, when you have something to solve it with.

Week 1. Learn to read a table

Days 1–2
What a model is, and one customer row. Variable types on the bank file: marital, education, balance, campaign.
Days 3–4
Mode, median, mean, SD. Why balance has a mean of €1,423 and a median of €444.
Days 5–6
SE = SD / √n, the 95% interval, and two claims: μ = 41 and μ = 44. Walk the bank_basics notebook line by line.

Week 2. Take the easy Python marks

Days 1–3
A name, a list, a set, a dictionary. The difference between = and ==, counting from zero, and a slice.
Days 4–6
if, for, a function, a nested dictionary. The four Olympics puzzles. Repeat df.head(25): that line is in the mock.

Week 3. What a fair experiment is

Days 1–2
Acquisition, the customer path, and KPIs. Privacy only as far as the slides go.
Days 3–6
The three conditions for a cause, X and O, selection bias, and the six design steps. The Fructis pack: 0.60 − 0.40 = 0.20.

Week 4. Artea, the whole case

Days 1–2
5,000 people, a coupon, and a check that the groups looked alike before the test.
Days 3–4
OLS: +0.03 transactions (p = 0.03) and no effect on revenue (p = 0.72).
Days 5–6
Interactions: Instagram and the abandoned cart. Who gets the coupon in the next campaign.

Week 5. A customer’s money, and a churn forecast

Days 1–2
CLV, and the example that moves churn from 20% to 15%: $1,676 versus $1,965.
Days 3–4
Which model matches which Y. OLS, logit, Poisson, hazard. Do not study a procedure the slide only names.
Days 5–6
A 75/25 calibration and validation split. Lift: the top group is 3 times the average. Overfitting.

Week 6. Logit the way they ask it

Days 1–2
Odds, the odds ratio, and (OR − 1) × 100. Never call that “percentage points of probability”.
Days 3–5
The whole Advertising lab, steps 1–8, with the notebook.
Day 6
The closing-class table: Marketing Spend, Female, Discount, Mobile. Then interactions.

Week 7. Two cases and matching

Days 1–2
Pilgrim: the offer lowered retention. Compute the OR from −0.1008. Do not treat “outside the test” as the control.
Days 3–4
Books R Us: the MNF letter is about €1, while “already multichannel” is about €33. Those are different questions.
Days 5–6
Sample Box: a raw gap of €199, about €41 after matching, and a pilot that sits below break-even.

Week 8. The exam, on a timer

Day 1
The format, the marks, attending versus non-attending, and what to study.
Days 2–3
The mock, part 1, 75 minutes on a timer, solutions closed.
Day 4
Open the solutions and check every sentence: the model, significance, odds rather than probability, and causation.
Days 5–6
A second pass of the mock, out loud. The gaps: papers that are not in the folder. The formula sheet again.

What they almost certainly ask

Read a table

OLS: the coefficient is in units of Y. Logit: exp(coefficient) is the odds ratio. Compare the p-value with 0.05, and in the mock also with 0.10.

Do not mix the questions

“Who looks likely to leave” and “who this offer will keep” are different models. A historical association and a random field test are different too.

Python, one mark

A short piece of code. In the sample mock they ask what head(25) does.
